How to read this map

Our work shows what we are building and how it connects. Roles & circles shows who carries responsibilities. A circle organizes roles around a shared purpose.

Six questions to keep the work in perspective

David Allen’s Horizons of Focus help us review our commitments. They are perspectives, not ranks in an organization. A program can have goals, ongoing responsibilities, and projects at the same time.

  1. H5 · Purpose & principlesWhy do we exist, and what do we stand for?

    The reason for the work and the values that guide it.

  2. H4 · VisionWhat future are we working toward?

    A picture of success over the longer term.

  3. H3 · GoalsWhat outcomes are we aiming for?

    Results to achieve over the next year or two.

  4. H2 · Areas of responsibilityWhat needs ongoing care?

    Work such as maintaining the website or supporting chapters.

  5. H1 · ProjectsWhat would “done” look like?

    A finishable outcome that takes more than one action.

  6. Ground · Next actionsWhat can someone do next?

    A concrete step. Next actions are tracked outside Atlas.

Responsibility and authority

In Holacracy, a role’s purpose explains what it serves; accountabilities describe ongoing responsibilities. A Domain is something the role has explicit authority to control, subject to relevant policies. Being responsible for a project does not by itself grant that authority.

Atlas currently records work ownership and descriptive scope. These entries alone do not establish formal Holacracy Domains or policies. Missing definitions are gaps to resolve, not implied permission requirements.
